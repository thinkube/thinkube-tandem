= Thinkube Tandem
:page-type: concept

_An experimental intent-driven development methodology for working with Claude Code, still changing. You say what you want in your own words; the machine grounds it in the real code, builds it under checks it cannot fake, and hands you a delivery to accept or refuse._

.TL;DR
Open the Tandem space in Thinkube IDE and type sentences: "Finished tasks stop crowding the ones I still have to do." Press *Read these N*, *Keep these N*, *Group into things to build*, then *Build the first*. Tandem works out which files each sentence lands in and what must become true, shows you exactly that, and asks you to sign it. Workers build it on a branch, other workers check it blind, and the report comes back with its proofs. You go on, take it back out, or run it again. Claude Code can put the sentences in the box for you; it cannot press anything after that.

== The decision

Most of what goes wrong between an idea and a change in production is translation: the sentence becomes a ticket, the ticket becomes criteria, the criteria become code, and at each step something is lost or invented. Tandem keeps the sentence. Your words are stored verbatim and are never machine-edited; everything else, the files, the checks, the code, is derived from them, and shown to you before anything is built. Two decisions are always yours and never the machine's:

. *Sign.* *Build these N* freezes exactly what you saw into signed work. Nothing is built before this.
. *Decide on the delivery.* *Go on to the next*, *Take it back out*, *Accept it* or *Not this*. Nothing lands without this.

Between the two the machine works alone, and it is not trusted: the checks are written by workers that cannot see the implementation, completion is decided by a separate judge rather than by a worker's claim, files outside a worker's declared scope are reverted, and everything that could not be delivered is written on the report.

Tandem is experimental. Its pages describe it as it is today, and it is still changing.

== What it saves you

* *No tickets.* You write the sentence a user would say. The promises are derived, in front of you, with the choices the machine made listed for you to replace.
* *No reading of diffs to know whether it worked.* The report carries its proofs: which checks ran, which passed, what a reviewer judged, and what only you can confirm on the running product.
* *No silent scope creep.* What a worker touches outside its declared scope is reverted.
* *No merge you did not choose.* The branch waits for your decision.
* *No post-mortems.* Every defect a run catches is one row in a ledger that outlives the space; xref:defect-ledger.adoc[the defect ledger] is how the method learns.

== The page's words and the engine's

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| On the page | Underneath

| a line in the box, *your sentences* | an *ask*: your words, kept verbatim, never machine-edited
| a thing to build (*first*, *then*, *later*) | a *set* of *units*: changes clustered by code coupling
| a promise on *what it will do* | a *change*: one sentence, the files it lands in, its acceptance criteria
| a line under a promise, *checked when it is built* | an *acceptance criterion* and the *probe* that proves it, written blind
| *only you can see this* / *Nobody could settle these* | an *observation*: a criterion only the running product can show
| the cut review, *Build these N* | the *cut*; signing mints a *TEP* bound to what you saw
| a card on *the run* | a *unit*: a coder, its checks, the tests brought under, or the closing *gate*
| *needs you* | a worker parked on a question about intent; the *supervisor* answered every other kind
| *The closing gate* | the gate: every check re-run clean, the *honesty scan* for self-declared deferrals, the repository's own suite
| *What the work noticed, and did not do* | *findings*: what the machine saw and could not settle
| *Report* | the *delivery*: a branch plus its proofs
|===

== Limits

* Keeping, choosing, signing and accepting are yours; Tandem's tools refuse them to Claude Code by design.
* A run lives in the Thinkube IDE session that started it; closing or reloading that window ends the run, and *Run it again* resumes from what was committed.
* Tandem is still changing; a page and the product can differ for a while after a release.
