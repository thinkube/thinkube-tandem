= Thinkube Tandem
:page-type: concept

_An experimental intent-driven development methodology for working with Claude Code, still changing. You say what you want in your own words; the machine grounds it in the real code, builds it under checks it cannot fake, and hands you a delivery to accept or refuse._

.TL;DR
Open the Tandem space in Thinkube IDE and type sentences: "Finished tasks stop crowding the ones I still have to do." Press *Read*, *Keep*, *Group*, then *Build the first*. Tandem works out which files each sentence lands in and what must become true, shows you exactly that, and asks you to sign it. Workers build it on a branch, other workers check it blind, and the delivery comes back with its proofs. You accept it, or you do not. Claude Code can put the sentences in the box for you; it cannot press anything after that.

== The decision

Most of what goes wrong between an idea and a change in production is translation: the sentence becomes a ticket, the ticket becomes criteria, the criteria become code, and at each step something is lost or invented. Tandem keeps the sentence. Your words are stored verbatim and are never machine-edited; everything else, the files, the acceptance criteria, the tests, the code, is derived from them, and shown to you before anything is built. Two decisions are always yours and never the machine's:

. *Sign.* Freezing exactly what you saw into a signed unit of work. Nothing is built before this.
. *Accept.* Merging a delivery whose proofs are green. Nothing lands without this.

Between the two the machine works alone, and it is not trusted: the checks are written by workers that cannot see the implementation, completion is decided by a separate judge rather than by a worker's claim, files outside a worker's declared footprint are reverted, and everything that could not be delivered is written on the delivery's face.

Tandem is experimental. Its pages describe it as it is today, and it is still changing.

== What it saves you

* *No tickets.* You write the sentence a user would say. The criteria are derived, in front of you, with the choices the machine made listed for you to replace.
* *No reading of diffs to know whether it worked.* The delivery carries its proofs: which checks ran, which passed, what a reviewer judged, and what only you can confirm on the running product.
* *No silent scope creep.* A worker's footprint is declared; what it touches outside it is reverted.
* *No merge you did not choose.* The branch waits for your accept.

== The vocabulary

ask:: Your words, kept verbatim. Everything derives from asks; asks are never machine-edited.
change:: One grounded intention: a sentence, the files it lands in, and the acceptance criteria that prove it.
unit:: Changes clustered by code coupling; the thing you add to a cut.
cut:: The set of changes you are about to freeze.
TEP:: A signed cut, named `TEP-<you>-<n>`, bound by a cryptographic token to exactly what you saw when you signed.
slice:: The engine's dispatch unit inside a TEP: one coder, its held-out probe authors, a footprint.
delivery:: A branch plus its proofs, offered for your accept.
finding:: Something the machine noticed, could not settle, and has no actor left to route to. It rides the delivery for you to weigh; it never blocks the accept.
observation:: An acceptance criterion only the running product can show. Carried for you to confirm by using the thing, never graded green by the machine.
project:: One repository, or one subtree of it, with a minted identity that your spaces hang from; see xref:identity.adoc[Projects and identity].

== Limits

* Keeping, choosing, signing and accepting are yours; Tandem's tools refuse them to Claude Code by design.
* A run lives in the Thinkube IDE session that started it; closing or reloading that window ends the run, and *Run it again* resumes from what was committed.
* Tandem is still changing; a page and the product can differ for a while after a release.
