= Taking up what the work noticed
:page-type: tutorial

_The reviewers see things nobody asked for. This is how those become the next sentences, without pushing in front of what you chose to build first._

.TL;DR
On a report, tick the findings you want under *What the work noticed, and did not do* and press *Keep the N selected for later*. When the things you chose are built, press *Put these N in the box* under *Kept for later*, then *Read these N*, *Keep these N*, *Group the rest*, *Build the first*. What you have afterwards is a new thing built from what the reviewers found, with the things you built first untouched.

== What you will learn

* What a finding is and why it never blocks a delivery.
* How to keep a finding without turning it into work yet.
* How kept findings become sentences, and how the new sentences are grouped without touching what is built.

== What you need

* A space with at least one delivered thing. xref:getting-started.adoc[Your first thing built] gets you there.

== 1. Read what the work noticed

A reviewer judging the running product has one question about each thing it sees that nobody asked for: does this stop the work being delivered? If it does, it is fixed as part of the work. If it does not, it is written down as a finding, and the delivery goes ahead. That is why the section is called *What the work noticed, and did not do*.

Each finding has a box to tick, what was seen, and under it *Ask:* followed by the sentence a person would say to want it fixed. On the todo app's third report, the reviewers found accents missing in the Spanish and Catalan labels and a delete confirmation that never names the task.

== 2. Keep the ones you want

Keeping records that you want a finding without making it work yet, so the things you chose to build first keep their place. Tick the findings you want. The button under the list changes from *Select what to keep for later* to *Keep the N selected for later*. Press it. Each finding now reads *kept for later* where it stands, and a section *Kept for later* appears under the report, naming the cut that noticed each one.

*Kept for later* gathers what you kept from every cut of the space, so you can keep from one report, then another, and take them all up at once.

image::tandem-15-kept-for-later.png[A report with two findings kept for later, and under it the Kept for later section gathering six from two cuts, with Put these 6 in the box]

== 3. Put them in the box

When the discoveries are what comes next, one press turns all of them into lines. Under *Kept for later*, press *Put these N in the box*. Each finding now reads *in the box* on its report, the strip says *N new lines written, not read*, and the box holds one drafted ask per line.

image::tandem-16-box-from-kept.png[The box with the six drafted asks, one per line, and Read these 6 on the strip]

== 4. Read, keep, and group the rest

From here the lines are sentences like any other: *Read these N* shows how the machine understood them, *Keep these N* makes them part of the record. The strip then offers *Group the rest* and names the new sentences by number. The things already built stay exactly as they are; only the new sentences need a thing to belong to.

image::tandem-17-group-the-rest.png[After Group the rest: four new things carrying only the new sentences, above the three accepted ones]

== 5. Build the first

Press *Build the first*. The path is the one from xref:getting-started.adoc#_7_build_the_first[step 7 of Your first thing built]: the thing is worked out, you sign, the run builds it, and the report comes back.

== What you have now

The reviewers' findings, in the record as your sentences, with the ones you chose built and the rest waiting under *then*. Nothing you built first was rebuilt or reread.

== Limits

* A finding you do not keep stays on its report; a later reviewer may report it again.
* The lines put in the box are in the reviewer's words; change them in the box before you read if you want them in yours.
